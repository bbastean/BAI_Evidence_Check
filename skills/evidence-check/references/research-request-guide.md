# Writing a Good Research Request

This guide is for the person asking for research. Claude reads it only when it needs to show a user how to frame a request.

## Be more specific about

- The exact metrics or data points you need ("market size in USD," "annual growth rate," "number of jurisdictions")
- Time periods. Precise dates matter for fast-moving topics.
- The organizations, products, places or people to cover
- Technical terms or jargon in your field
- Regulatory frameworks or standards that apply
- Known controversies or competing viewpoints you want examined
- Your confidence threshold (for example, "only VERIFIED and SUPPORTED findings in the executive summary")

## Be less specific about

- The exact sources to use. Give categories and quality requirements and let the research find the best ones.
- How to organize the report, unless you have a strong preference
- Supporting examples, if "a few good ones" is all you need

## Example request

```
Research: What does the published evidence show about four-day workweek
trials and productivity, and do the effects last beyond the trial period?

Context & Scope:
- Why I'm researching this: Advising a 200-person company considering a pilot
- Time frame: Trials and studies published 2019 to present
- Geographic scope: Any country, but note where results come from
- Depth: Comprehensive; I need to present this to a leadership team

Specific Information Needs:
1. Which large trials have published results, who ran them, and their sample sizes
2. What productivity, retention and wellbeing measures were used
3. Results at the end of each trial and at any later follow-up
4. Which sectors or job types saw weaker or negative results
5. Methodological criticisms of the trials (self-selection, self-reported data)

Source Requirements:
- Required: Peer-reviewed studies, official trial reports from the organizing
  researchers, government evaluations
- Acceptable: Major news coverage, reputable business press, academic commentary
- Leads only: Encyclopedias, advocacy summaries, AI-generated overviews
- Exclude: Vendor or consultancy marketing pages presented as research

Confidence Requirements:
- Only VERIFIED or SUPPORTED findings in the executive summary
- Flag any result that rests on self-reported data
- List trials I should know about that have no published results yet

Output Format:
- Executive summary
- Table of trials: organizer, country, sample size, duration, measures, headline result
- Dissenting views and methodological critiques, held to the same standard
- What Was Not Found
```

A request like this gives the research a clear question, a reason (which shapes what matters), named measures, a source policy and a stated confidence bar. It also asks for the critics' case directly, which the framework would look for anyway.
